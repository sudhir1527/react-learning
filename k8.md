# Swing Trading Curriculum: Revised (Indian Equity Markets)
## Beginner to Strong. Topics to Study, in Order

> **Education only, not investment advice.** No curriculum can guarantee profit. Most retail traders lose money, so the goal here is to build a tested process and protect your capital while you learn.

## 2. Revised Curriculum

### Phase 0: Setup and Realistic Expectations [NEW] (Week 1)
- Why most retail traders lose money, and what a realistic first year looks like (learning, not earning).
- Keep trading capital separate from money you need for living costs, emergencies or goals.
- Open and understand your demat and trading account. Compare brokerages and charges.
- Charting and screening tools: a charting platform (for example TradingView), a free screener (for example Chartink or Screener.in), and the NSE/BSE websites for announcements and data.
- Start a **trade and observation journal on day one** (a spreadsheet is enough).

**Milestone:** Tools are set up, the journal exists, and you have written down your learning goal and the maximum money you could ever afford to lose.

---

### Phase 1: Market Foundations and Indian Market Mechanics [CORE, EXPANDED]
**From the original**
- Exchanges, brokers, indices, participants. NSE, BSE, Nifty 50, Nifty 500, sector indices.
- OHLC, volume, bid-ask spread, liquidity, order-book depth, volatility.
- Order types: market, limit, stop-market, stop-limit. Trade-offs of each.
- Corporate actions: dividends, splits, bonuses, buybacks, earnings.
- Long vs short, margin and leverage risks.

**Added for India [NEW]**
- **Product types:** delivery (CNC) vs intraday (MIS) vs F&O. Swing trades are normally **delivery** trades.
- **Settlement:** T+1 cycle, what BTST means and its risks (including the auction risk when shares are not delivered).
- **Price bands and circuit limits:** upper and lower circuits, and how they can trap you in a position.
- **Exchange surveillance lists:** ASM and GSM frameworks, trade-to-trade segment, and why you avoid these stocks.
- **F&O ban list** and why it matters for stocks you watch.
- **Short selling reality:** in the cash segment you cannot hold a short position overnight. Short-side swing trading means futures, which is a later topic [DEFER].
- **Costs:** brokerage, STT, exchange charges, SEBI fees, GST, stamp duty, DP charges on sells. Check your broker's current charge sheet, and build costs into every test.
- **Taxes on swing trades:** at the time of writing (FY 2026-27), listed equity held 12 months or less with STT paid is taxed as short-term capital gains at 20% (plus applicable surcharge and cess). Whether very frequent trading is treated as capital gains or business income depends on your facts. Learn advance tax and the ITR form basics, and **confirm the current rules with a chartered accountant**, since rates and sections change.
- Pre-open session, market hours, and what opening gaps are.

**Practice:** Read daily charts of 20 liquid large-cap stocks. Compare a market order and a limit order on a real chart. Calculate total round-trip cost on a sample trade.

**Milestone:** Explain how an order is filled, why the fill price can differ from what you expected, and what a trade actually costs you after charges and tax.

### Phase 2: Chart Reading and Market Structure [CORE]
- Candlestick anatomy (only the basics here; patterns come later).
- Uptrend (higher highs, higher lows), downtrend, range.
- Support and resistance as **zones**, role reversal, swing highs and lows.
- Trendlines and channels. Breakouts, breakdowns, retests, failed breakouts.
- Dow Theory basics. Multi-timeframe: weekly for context, daily for entries.
- Stage analysis (Weinstein) as a way to label where a stock is in its cycle.

**Practice:** Mark trend and levels on 30 historical charts. Label each as up, down or range. Save failed-breakout examples.

**Milestone:** Describe a chart's structure without any indicator.

---

### Phase 3: Risk Basics [CORE, NEW POSITION: MOVED from original Phase 9]
Study this in Month 2 and use it in every practice trade from then on.
- Risk per trade as a percentage of account (your choice, and keep it small while learning).
- Stop placement based on structure and volatility (ATR), not on a rupee amount you "can afford".
- Position size = maximum rupee risk / (entry - stop). Calculate it before every practice trade.
- R-multiples: measure every trade as a multiple of the initial risk.
- Gaps and slippage: your real loss can exceed your planned loss.

**Milestone:** You never log a practice trade without entry, stop, size and risk in rupees.

---

### Phase 4: Market Regime and Indian Market Context [CORE, EXPANDED]
- Bull, bear and sideways regimes. Nifty 50 and Nifty 500 trend.
- Market breadth (advancing vs declining, share of stocks above key moving averages).
- Sector strength and rotation (Nifty Bank, IT, Auto, Pharma, and so on).
- Volatility: expansion and contraction. **India VIX [NEW]**.
- **FII and DII flow data [NEW]:** how to read it and its limits (context, not a signal).
- **Global and macro context [NEW]:** GIFT Nifty, US markets, crude, USD/INR, RBI policy dates, Union Budget.
- When to trade normally, reduce size, or stand aside.

**Practice:** Check the index and sector before looking at any stock. Classify past weeks by regime and compare your practice results across regimes.

**Milestone:** State which market conditions each of your intended setups is designed for.

---

### Phase 5: Stock Selection and Screening [CORE]
- Liquidity filters (average traded value and volume).
- Relative strength vs Nifty and vs sector.
- Bases, consolidations, orderly pullbacks. Volume confirmation.
- Volatility and whether a sensible stop is practical.
- Basic fundamental quality screen: sales and profit growth, debt, cash flow.
- **Red flags [NEW]:** high promoter pledging, ASM/GSM status, frequent circuit hits, thin liquidity, hype-driven moves.
- Building and maintaining a watchlist of 20 to 30 liquid stocks.
- Checking results dates and corporate announcements before planning a trade.

**Milestone:** A repeatable shortlist produced by written rules, not by browsing.

---

### Phase 6: Price Action, Volume and Patterns [PRIORITISED]
**[CORE]**
- Strong-range candles, inside bars, engulfing patterns, hammer and shooting star **in context** (at support, resistance, or trend pullbacks).
- Volume expansion and contraction, breakout volume vs normal volume.
- **Delivery percentage [NEW]** as supporting context for Indian stocks (not proof of accumulation).
- Flags, pennants, rectangles, triangles, base-and-breakout structures.
- Double tops and bottoms.

**[OPTIONAL]**
- Head and shoulders, wedges, gaps (breakaway, continuation, exhaustion).
- Morning star, evening star, doji, marubozu, three-methods patterns.
- Supply and demand zones.

**[DEFER]**
- Volume Profile, Point of Control, Value Area.
- Volume divergence analysis.

**Rule:** A pattern is not a signal. Location, trend, regime, liquidity and your tested rules give it meaning. Study pattern statistics (for example Bulkowski's research) to see how often patterns fail.

**Milestone:** For any pattern, state its context and what invalidates it.

---

### Phase 7: Indicators and Volatility [PRIORITISED]
**[CORE]**
- 20/50/200 moving averages (SMA and EMA) for trend context.
- ATR for stop distance and position sizing.
- RSI, including how it behaves in strong trends (it can stay "overbought" for weeks).
- Volume-based confirmation (from Phase 6).

**[OPTIONAL]**
- MACD, Bollinger Bands.
- Fibonacci retracements (as a rough zone, not a precise level).

**[DEFER]**
- Stochastic, ROC, Keltner Channels, Fibonacci extensions, divergence strategies.

**Rule:** Add one indicator at a time and test whether it improves your written rules. Do not stack indicators that measure the same thing.

**Milestone:** Explain exactly what each indicator in your system contributes.

---

### Phase 8: Fundamentals, Catalysts and Event Risk [CORE, EXPANDED]
- Revenue, earnings, margins, debt, cash flow, valuation basics.
- Quarterly results and the **results-season calendar**. Decide your rule: hold through results or avoid them.
- Management commentary and guidance.
- **Indian event calendar [NEW]:** RBI policy, Union Budget, state elections and general election periods, index rebalancing, F&O expiry weeks.
- **Corporate disclosures [NEW]:** bulk and block deals, promoter buying or selling, pledging changes, insider-trading disclosures, regulatory actions.
- Corporate actions and **adjusted prices** when backtesting.

**Milestone:** A written event-risk rule you follow before every trade.

---

### Phase 9: Core Swing Setups [CORE]
Learn **two or three** setups deeply. Do not collect setups.
- **Setup A:** Breakout from a consolidation.
- **Setup B:** Pullback in an established uptrend.
- **Setup C:** Breakout and retest.

**For each setup, write down:**
1. Market conditions required.
2. Stock-selection rules.
3. Entry trigger.
4. Initial stop and why it sits there.
5. Target or exit logic (see Phase 10).
6. Position-size calculation.
7. Invalidation conditions.
8. Historical evidence.

**[DEFER]:** range trading, reversal trading, countertrend, gap strategies, short-side strategies.

**Milestone:** At least one setup written precisely enough that another person could follow it and reach the same trade.

---

### Phase 10: Trade Management and Exits [CORE, NEW]
Study this alongside Phase 9. Your exit rules shape your results as much as your entries.
- Initial stop vs trailing stop (structure-based, moving-average-based, ATR-based).
- Fixed targets vs trailing exits vs partial profit-taking, and how each changes expectancy.
- Time stops: exiting a trade that goes nowhere.
- Moving a stop to breakeven: when it helps and when it just gets you stopped out.
- Handling overnight gaps and earnings gaps.
- Stop orders in practice: stop-market vs stop-limit, and the GTT or bracket facilities your broker offers.
- Never widening a stop after entry.

**Milestone:** Every setup has a written exit plan covering profit, loss and time.

---

### Phase 11: Portfolio Risk and Performance Statistics [CORE]
- Win rate, average win, average loss, expectancy (in R), profit factor.
- Maximum drawdown and losing streaks, and how to size so a streak does not end you.
- Correlation, sector concentration, total open risk across all positions.
- Rules for reducing size after a drawdown.
- Why risk-reward ratios alone do not make a strategy profitable.

**Milestone:** You can read your trade log and say whether your edge is real, flat or negative.

---

### Phase 12: Psychology, Discipline and Journaling [CORE]
- FOMO, chasing, loss aversion, revenge trading, confirmation bias, overtrading.
- Pre-trade checklist and written trading plan.
- Separating a good process from a good outcome.
- Journal fields: date, ticker, regime, setup, entry, stop, target, size, planned vs actual risk, costs, rules followed (yes/no), screenshot, lesson.
- Weekly review of every rule violation.

**Milestone:** Your journal shows a high rule-following rate, regardless of whether trades won.

---

### Phase 13: Backtesting, Validation and Live Transition [CORE, EXPANDED]
**Manual backtesting**
- Unambiguous rules. Record **every** qualifying setup, not only attractive ones.
- No future information. Use adjusted prices and include costs, tax and slippage.

**Sample size [NEW]**
- A few trades prove nothing. Treat about 30 trades as a bare minimum for any statistic to be meaningful, and 100 or more as far better, per setup.
- Test across different years and regimes, and across many stocks, not one or two.

**Testing errors to avoid**
- Look-ahead bias, survivorship bias, overfitting, data errors, selection bias.

**Validation sequence**
1. Write rules. 2. Backtest. 3. Freeze rules and test on a separate period (out-of-sample / walk-forward). 4. Paper trade forward. 5. Review execution and rule adherence.

**Small live transition [NEW]**
- Paper trading cannot reproduce real fills or real emotions. Only after steps 1 to 5, consider live trading with **very small risk per trade** (an amount you would not mind losing).
- Set rules in advance: a maximum loss that pauses you, and a minimum number of live trades before judging the system.
- Reduce size, not rules, when you hit a drawdown.

**Milestone:** A written report on the strategy's strengths, weaknesses, drawdowns and limitations.

---

### Phase 14: Advanced Development [DEFER]
Only after Phase 13 is complete and your process is consistent.
- Python for data analysis, screening and backtesting.
- Robustness testing and parameter sensitivity.
- Monte Carlo analysis of drawdowns.
- Portfolio-level exposure and correlation analysis.
- Trailing-stop and exit comparisons at scale.
- F&O, options hedging and short-side strategies (with their own risk education).

Automation should support a tested process, not make an untested one look scientific.

## 3. Revised 12-Month Schedule

| Period | Focus | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | Phase 0 and Phase 1 (mechanics, costs, tax basics). Start chart reading and the journal | Tools set up; journal running |
| Month 2 | Phase 2 (chart structure) and **Phase 3 (risk basics)** | Annotated charts; every practice trade has a size and stop |
| Month 3 | Phase 4 (regime and Indian market context) | Weekly regime classification |

| Month 5 | Phase 6 (price action, volume, patterns) | Patterns recognised in context |
| Month 6 | Phase 7 (core indicators and ATR) | Evidence of what each indicator adds |
| Month 7 | Phase 8 (fundamentals, catalysts, event risk) | Event-risk checklist |
| Months 8 to 9 | Phase 9 and Phase 10 (setups and exits) | Written setup and exit rules |

Paper-trade and journal from Month 2 onward, so you accumulate trades while you study. Extend any phase where you cannot meet its milestone. The timeline is a guide, not a promise of profitability.

## 4. Daily and Weekly Routine

**Daily (after market close, about 45 to 60 minutes) [NEW]**
1. Check Nifty and sector trend, India VIX, and any major news.
2. Run your screener and update the watchlist.
3. Mark levels and plan orders for the next session. Write entry, stop, size and exit before placing anything.
4. Update the journal for open and closed trades.

**Weekly (7 to 10 hours total)**
- 2 hours: concept study and notes.
- 2 hours: historical chart review.
- 2 hours: screening and setup identification practice.
- 1 to 2 hours: backtesting and journaling.
- 1 hour: review mistakes and rule violations.

## 5. Learning Resources

**Free and structured**
- **Zerodha Varsity** (https://zerodha.com/varsity/): technical analysis, risk management, markets and taxation basics, trading psychology.
- **SEBI Investor Education** (https://investor.sebi.gov.in/): investor protection, risks and safeguards.
- **NSE and BSE websites:** announcements, corporate actions, surveillance lists, market data.

**Books** (they are mostly US-based, so test every idea on Indian data)
- John J. Murphy, *Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets*: the standard reference for charting. [CORE]
- Stan Weinstein, *Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets*: stage analysis and trend context. [CORE]
- William O'Neil, *How to Make Money in Stocks*: stock selection, relative strength, breakouts. [CORE]
- Mark Minervini, *Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard*: trend-template and risk discipline. [OPTIONAL]
- Van K. Tharp, *Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom*: expectancy, R-multiples and position sizing. [CORE]
- Mark Douglas, *Trading in the Zone*: psychology. [CORE]
- Thomas Bulkowski, *Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns*: statistics on how patterns actually perform. [OPTIONAL]

## 6. Final Checklist: Ready for Live Money?

- [ ] I can explain the regime and why the setup suits it.
- [ ] My stock-selection, entry, stop and exit rules are written and objective.
- [ ] I calculate position size before every trade.
- [ ] I know the full cost of a trade, including charges and tax.
- [ ] I recorded all setups, including losers, with enough trades to be meaningful.
- [ ] I tested across multiple market conditions and checked for look-ahead bias and overfitting.
- [ ] I have a written event-risk rule (results, RBI, Budget).
- [ ] I paper-traded the frozen rules and my journal shows consistent rule-following.
- [ ] I have defined my maximum loss, my pause rule and my live-trading size limit.
- [ ] I can afford the possible loss without hurting essential financial needs.

**Final principle:** The aim is not to predict every move. It is to follow a repeatable, tested process, control risk, and let evidence tell you whether your setups have an edge.
